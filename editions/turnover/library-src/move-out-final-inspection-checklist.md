### Post-Inspection: Security Deposit & Owner
---
Damage Assessment
☐ Compare all flagged items to the move-in report - confirm tenant damage vs. normal wear and tear
☐ Calculate estimated repair and cleaning costs for all chargeable items
☐ Reference the pre-established fee schedule for standard charges (repainting, carpet cleaning, etc.)
☐ Obtain vendor invoices or estimates for any repairs exceeding standard fees
#### Security Deposit Disposition
☐ Calculate total deductions (damages + cleaning + unpaid balances)
☐ Prepare itemized security deposit disposition letter for tenant
☐ Send disposition letter and remaining deposit within the legally required timeframe for the state
☐ Upload disposition letter and all supporting photos and invoices to the platform
☐ Log tenant forwarding address in the system
#### Owner Communication
☐ Notify owner of move-out inspection results
☐ Share full inspection report and photos with owner via owner portal
☐ Notify owner of any significant damages requiring approval before repair
☐ Discuss make-ready timeline and next steps for re-leasing
#### Make-Ready Initiation
☐ Create work orders for all required repairs and cleaning
☐ Assign to in-house techs or vendors as appropriate
☐ Confirm make-ready timeline with owner
☐ Begin marketing prep once make-ready timeline is confirmed
#### Final Documentation
☐ Upload complete move-out inspection report to the platform
☐ Upload all photos to tenant and owner portals
☐ Mark all move-out tasks complete in the system
☐ Rekey locks (always, regardless of key return)
### Manager Notes

---
Always compare to the move-in report side by side; without that baseline a damage charge is hard to defend. Normal wear and tear is not chargeable - small nail holes, minor scuffs, and normally worn carpet are the owner's cost of doing business, and charging for them is the top cause of deposit disputes. Photo everything, including what is fine - a clean photo of a pristine wall proves condition. Rekey every time without exception. A tenant told in writing what to fix who chose not to fix it is a much cleaner deduction to defend.

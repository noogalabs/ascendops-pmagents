## Turn-Time & Make-Ready Budget Benchmarks by Property Class

Purpose: grade every turnover the same way you grade maintenance, with clear, class-specific benchmarks for turn-time, budget, and quality so you can measure performance, spot problems early, and report meaningfully to owners.
### How Property Classes Are Defined

---

| Class | Age | Condition | Rent Range | Tenant Profile | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Class A | Built within ~15 years | Excellent, premium finishes, modern amenities | Top 20% of market | High-income professionals | New construction, luxury SFR, high-end condos |

### Turn-Time Benchmarks by Class

---
Turn-time = calendar days from tenant move-out to unit rent-ready (marketing live). This is your primary vacancy cost control metric.

| Class | Green (On Target) | Yellow (Watch) | Red (Escalate) | Industry Average |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Class A | <= 10 days | 11-15 days | 16+ days | 10-14 days |
| Class B | <= 12 days | 13-18 days | 19+ days | 12-16 days |
| Class C | <= 14 days | 15-21 days | 22+ days | 14-21 days |
| Class D | <= 21 days | 22-30 days | 31+ days | 21-35 days |

#### Why classes differ
Class A: higher owner expectations, premium market moves fast, finishes require precision but vendors are readily available
Class B: balanced scope, moderate complexity, most common portfolio type
Class C: more deferred maintenance discovered at move-out, older systems, more scope creep risk
Class D: heaviest repair burden, Section 8 inspections add time, vendor availability can be limited

### The Turnover Grading Scorecard

---
Use this to grade every completed turnover, just like you grade maintenance work orders. Score each category and calculate a final grade.
#### Scoring Rubric

| Category | Weight | 4 pts | 3 pts | 2 pts | 1 pt |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Turn-Time | 30% | At or under Green benchmark | 1-3 days over Green | In Yellow range | In Red range |
| Budget Adherence | 25% | At or under Green benchmark | Up to 10% over Green | In Yellow range | In Red range |
| Inspection Quality | 20% | Zero items flagged at final inspection | 1 minor item flagged | 2-3 items flagged | Failed final inspection |
| Owner Communication | 15% | All 3 emails sent on time with no owner follow-up needed | 1 email late or owner had to follow up once | 2 emails late or owner followed up twice | Owner had to chase updates repeatedly |
| Documentation | 10% | All photos, invoices, and reports uploaded same day as completion | Uploaded within 1 day | Uploaded within 3 days | Missing or uploaded 4+ days late |

#### Final Grade Calculation

| Score | Grade | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 3.7-4.0 | A | Exceptional, this is the standard |
| 3.0-3.6 | B | Good, minor improvements needed |
| 2.0-2.9 | C | Needs improvement, review with team |
| 1.0-1.9 | D | Poor, escalate and root cause immediately |

### Manager Notes

---
Vacancy cost is your most underreported metric. A Class B unit at $1,500/month sitting vacant for 5 extra days costs the owner $250 in lost rent, often more than the savings from a cheaper vendor. Turn-time IS a financial metric.
Class D properties need their own vendor relationships. Standard vendors often won't prioritize lower-rent properties. Build a dedicated Class C/D vendor list with contractors who specialize in that work and can move fast.
Separate tenant damage costs from make-ready costs in your reporting. If a tenant caused $800 in damage, that's a charge-back, not a make-ready cost. Mixing them inflates your benchmarks and makes your team look less efficient than they are.
A turnover grade below B two months in a row on the same property is a signal: either the property has a systemic maintenance issue, the owner is slow to approve, or your vendor isn't performing. Investigate before it becomes a pattern.
Use Yellow as your early warning system. Don't wait for Red to act. A Yellow score is your chance to course-correct before it costs the owner real money.

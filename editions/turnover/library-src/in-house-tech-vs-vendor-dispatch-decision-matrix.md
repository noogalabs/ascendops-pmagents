# In-House Tech vs Vendor Dispatch Decision Matrix

## In-House Tech vs. Vendor Dispatch Decision Matrix
Maintenance Work Order Assignment — [Company Name]
Purpose: ensure every work order is assigned to the right resource, every time, based on scope, urgency, licensing requirements, cost, and risk. Eliminate guesswork and protect the company, owner, and tenant.
When in doubt, dispatch a licensed vendor. The cost of an unlicensed repair gone wrong far exceeds the cost of a vendor call.
### The Golden Rules - Read Before Using the Matrix

---

| Rule | Details |
|---|---|
| 1. License = Vendor | Any work requiring a license (electrical, plumbing, HVAC, gas) ALWAYS goes to a licensed vendor - no exceptions |
| 2. Safety = Vendor | Any repair that could affect tenant safety if done incorrectly goes to a vendor |
| 3. Emergency = Fastest Qualified Resource | In an emergency, dispatch whoever can respond fastest, in-house or vendor, but never sacrifice licensing requirements |
| 4. Cost Threshold = Owner Approval | Any repair exceeding the pre-approved spending threshold requires owner approval before dispatch |
| 5. Scope Creep = Stop & Reassess | If an in-house tech starts a job and discovers it is bigger than expected: stop, document, and reassign to a vendor |

### Step 1 - Categorize the Work Order First

---

| Priority | Definition | Response Time |
|---|---|---|
| EMERGENCY | Immediate threat to safety, health, or major property damage | Immediate - same day |
| URGENT | Significantly impacts tenant comfort or habitability | Within 24-48 hours |
| ROUTINE | Standard repair, does not impact habitability | Within 7-10 days |
| LOW PRIORITY | Cosmetic or minor, no habitability impact | Scheduled within 14 days |

### Step 2 - Run the Decision Tree

---
Does the repair require a license? (electrical, plumbing, HVAC, gas, structural)
    YES → VENDOR, no exceptions
NO ↓ Could it cause a safety risk if done incorrectly?
    YES → VENDOR
NO ↓ Is it an emergency requiring immediate response?
    YES → fastest QUALIFIED resource: in-house if within skill set, otherwise on-call emergency vendor
NO ↓ Is it within the in-house tech's confirmed skill set?
    NO → VENDOR
YES ↓ Does the estimated cost exceed the owner's pre-approved threshold?
    YES → owner approval first, then assign accordingly
NO →
    IN-HOUSE TECH
### Step 3 - The Full Decision Matrix
---
Emergency - Immediate Response

| Issue | Assign To | Why | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Active flooding / major water leak | VENDOR | Licensed plumber required | Shut off water first - in-house can do this |
| Gas leak | VENDOR | Licensed gas tech + utility company | Call 911 and gas company first - evacuate tenant |
| No heat in extreme cold | VENDOR | Licensed HVAC required | Habitability - do not delay |
| No AC in extreme heat | VENDOR | Licensed HVAC required | Habitability - do not delay |
| Electrical sparking / burning smell | VENDOR | Licensed electrician required | Call 911 if fire risk - never attempt in-house |
| Complete power outage (unit only) | IN-HOUSE FIRST | Check breaker panel first | If breaker reset does not resolve - vendor |
| Sewage backup | VENDOR | Licensed plumber required | Health hazard - immediate response |
| Broken exterior door / lock - security risk | IN-HOUSE or locksmith | Security issue | In-house if capable, locksmith vendor if not |
| Roof leak - active | VENDOR | Licensed roofer required | Document with photos immediately |
| Fire damage | VENDOR | Restoration company required | Call 911 first - notify owner immediately |

### Always Vendor - No Exceptions

---

| Category | Examples |
|---|---|
| Electrical | Panel work, wiring, outlet installation, GFCI replacement, any work behind the wall |
| Plumbing | Supply line work, drain line repair, water heater installation, pipe repair |

| Pest control | Always a licensed pest control company |
| Pool / spa | Always a licensed pool service company |
| Fire / water damage restoration | Always a licensed restoration company |

### Cost Threshold & Owner Approval Quick Reference

---

| Scenario | Action |
|---|---|
| Repair under pre-approved threshold | Dispatch without owner approval - notify after |
| Repair over pre-approved threshold | Get owner approval before dispatching |
| Emergency over threshold | Dispatch immediately to protect safety and property - notify owner simultaneously |
| Scope expands beyond original estimate | Stop work, get new estimate, notify owner before proceeding |
| Owner wants to use their own vendor | Confirm proof of license and insurance before allowing access |

### Dispatch Quick-Reference Card

---
Print and post at the maintenance coordinator's desk.

EMERGENCY? Safety risk or major damage → VENDOR (on-call list). No safety risk → in-house if qualified.
ELECTRICAL / PLUMBING / HVAC / GAS? Always VENDOR, no exceptions.
REKEYING? Always LOCKSMITH VENDOR.
ROUTINE REPAIR IN THE IN-HOUSE SKILL SET? IN-HOUSE TECH.
NOT SURE? VENDOR - always the safer call.
### Manager Notes

---
When in doubt, dispatch a vendor - the liability from an unlicensed repair that causes injury, fire, or flooding far exceeds any savings. Train in-house techs to know their limits; the best tech is the one who knows exactly when to stop and call a vendor. Keep the on-call emergency vendor list current - review quarterly for availability, licensing, and insurance. Document every dispatch decision: who, why, when, at what cost. Scope creep is the biggest in-house risk - stop, document, escalate, never improvise past the skill set.

### Section 2: Pipeline Stages with Entry and Exit Criteria
#### Stage 1: New Lead / Captured
Owner: BDM
Entry Criteria: Lead submits inquiry OR BDM initiates outbound contact (FRBO, referral, etc.)
Exit Criteria: BDM makes first live contact and qualifies the lead as a property management prospect (not brokerage/investment redirect)
BDM Actions:
- Log lead in CRM immediately with source, property address, contact info
- Respond within the configured {{speed_to_lead_minutes}}-minute window of an inbound inquiry (speed-to-lead is critical)
- Determine: Is this a PM lead, a brokerage lead, or an investment lead?
- If not PM → redirect to appropriate department; if PM → move to Stage 2
#### Stage 2: Discovery Call Scheduled
Owner: BDM
Entry Criteria: Live contact made; owner confirmed as PM prospect; call/meeting scheduled
Exit Criteria: Discovery call completed; rental analysis initiated; listing appointment set
BDM Actions:
- Conduct discovery call (see Section 3 for full question bank)
- Capture all property details in CRM
- Identify all decision-makers; ensure all will be present at listing appointment
- Send post-call email within 5 minutes of hanging up
- Send text within 15 minutes: "Great call today. I look forward to speaking with you on [DATE] at [TIME]. Be sure to check your email and save my contact info in your phone."
- Begin rental market analysis
#### Stage 3: Pre-Listing Appointment Nurture
Owner: BDM
Entry Criteria: Listing appointment is booked
Exit Criteria: Listing appointment occurs
BDM Actions (Multi-Touch Sequence):
- 24 hours before: Call: "Hi, this is [NAME]. I look forward to our meeting tomorrow at [TIME]. Just wanted to see if you had any questions. By the way, this is my cell; save me as [NAME] Property Manager."
- Morning of: Text: "Hi, this is [NAME]. Looking forward to meeting with you later today at [TIME]. Be sure to check your email 30 minutes before our call."
- Pre-qualify further: motivation, timeline, objections, who else they're interviewing, what matters most, will they sign at the appointment?
- Finalize rental rate range; get help from ops/market data if needed
- Confirm ownership entity (verify via public tax records: [STATE: insert county/tax record website])
---
Pro Tip: Frame your reminder call as "I'm calling to remind you," not "I'm calling to confirm." Confirmation invites cancellation. Reminder assumes it's happening.
#### Stage 4: Listing Appointment (Presentation)
Owner: BDM
Entry Criteria: All decision-makers present; rental analysis ready; pricing strategy prepared
Exit Criteria: PMA signed OR clear next step with hard follow-up date
Primary Goal: Get the signed Property Management Agreement (PMA)
Secondary Goal: If not signed, leave with a committed next step and date
BDM Actions:
- Present rental analysis and recommended rental rate
- Walk through services, packages, and pricing (see Section 5)
- Handle objections in real time (see Section 6)
- Use the 3-Part Close (see Section 5)
- If signed → immediately trigger post-PMA workflow (see Section 7)
- If not signed → set hard follow-up date before leaving the call
#### Stage 5: PMA Signed, Handoff Initiated
Owner: BDM → Operations / Onboarding Specialist
Entry Criteria: PMA fully executed by owner and authorized company signatory
Exit Criteria: Owner Intake Form received; onboarding specialist has made first contact; BDM formally handed off
BDM Actions (Day 1, Immediate):
- Send thank-you + next steps email (see Section 7)
- Send Owner Intake Form link (or offer to complete together on a call)
- Notify onboarding specialist via CRM task/automated trigger
- Schedule onboarding call within 48 hours
- Confirm ownership entity for PMA accuracy: [STATE: one PMA per unit may be required]
#### Stage 6: Active Onboarding (Operations Owns)
Owner: Onboarding Specialist / Customer Success
Entry Criteria: Owner Intake Form submitted; BDM handoff complete
Exit Criteria: Owner in PM software, all docs collected, setup fee paid, unit onboarding triggered, 90-day journey launched
(See Section 7 for full handoff package details)
### Section 3: Discovery Questions (BDM Master List)
#### Property Basics
- Where is the property located? (Address, city, state, zip)
- Tell me about the property: bedrooms, baths, square footage, stories, pool?
- Is this a primary residence, secondary home, or investment property?
- When did you purchase the property?
- Are you currently living there, or do you have a tenant in place?
#### Financial Picture
- What are your carrying costs: mortgage, insurance, taxes, HOA fees?
- What does the HOA include, if applicable?
- How much are you hoping to rent it for?
- Have you considered selling the property instead?
#### Motivation and Timeline
- Tell me: what are you looking to accomplish short-term and long-term with this property?
- How soon would you like us to place a tenant?
- How soon will the home be available?
- Have you ever rented this property, or any property, before?
- If yes, did you work with a property manager? What did you like or dislike?
#### Competitive Intelligence
- Have you spoken with any other property management companies?
- What did you like or dislike about them?
- Are they a dedicated property management company or a real estate agent doing PM on the side?
- Why are you leaving your current manager? (if applicable)
- What is most important to you in a property management company?
#### Decision-Making
- Are you working with a real estate agent on the purchase or sale of this property?
- Do you own any other properties?
- Are you currently buying, selling, or leasing any other properties?
- Who else will be involved in making this decision? (Spouse, partner, LLC member?)
- When do you plan to make a decision on hiring a property manager?
#### Qualifying Close
---
"Great, all we need to do is set an appointment so I can help you get this property rented, net you the most money, and protect your asset. Does [DAY/TIME] or [DAY/TIME] work better for you?"
### Section 4: Rental Rate Presentation
Use this during the listing appointment after completing your market analysis.
#### Script
---
"Mr./Mrs. [Owner], this is the estimate: the price your neighbors have rented their homes for in the past [X weeks/months]. Keep in mind, this is only one source we considered when maximizing your rental rate.
In your opinion, are these homes at all comparable to yours? Which ones are similar, and which are different?
So now that you've seen the competition and recently leased properties: what price do you feel we should use to create value in the eyes of a quality tenant?
Based on the information we reviewed, I'm going to recommend a price of $[AMOUNT]. Will you list your property with me at that price today?"
#### If the owner wants to start higher
---
"Here's the good news: we usually know within the first 7 days if we're priced correctly. If we're not, we immediately make the necessary adjustments to maximize your cash flow. Let's start at $[AMOUNT], and if we don't get enough activity or showings, we'll both know we need to adjust. Sound like a plan? Great: go ahead and sign the management agreement."
### Section 5: Management Fee Conversation and Close
#### Pricing Presentation Framework
---
"I can send you a full breakdown, but let me give you the overview here.
We offer three full-service packages ranging from $[LOW] to $[HIGH] per month per property. That covers everything associated with the tenant and the property: rent collection, all service calls, maintenance coordination, accounting, and lease enforcement.
We do charge a one-time setup fee of $[AMOUNT] to onboard your property into our accounting, maintenance, and management systems. We also hold a maintenance reserve of $[AMOUNT]: that's your money, not a fee. It allows us to act fast on repairs, which keeps your tenant happy and your property protected.
Our top package includes all of our guarantee programs: eviction protection, damage coverage above the security deposit, on-time owner payments even if the tenant pays late, and we waive the tenant placement fee entirely. It's a real no-brainer at $[AMOUNT]/month.
We've designed our pricing to be affordable, full-service, and give you total peace of mind."
#### The 3-Part Close
---
"Based on everything we've discussed, there are really only three parts to your decision; may I share what they are?
Part one: Do you absolutely want to rent out this property?
Part two: Are you comfortable with the pricing strategy we laid out?
Part three: Which property manager do you want handling this for you?
Here's the good news: you've already made your decision. You want to rent the property. We're aligned on pricing. And you feel comfortable with us. Why don't we do the right thing: sign the agreement today and get you one step closer to generating consistent monthly revenue. Wouldn't that be great?"
### Section 6: Objections with Word-for-Word Responses
#### "Your fees are too high / you charge more than the competition."
---
"I can see why you're concerned. But our clients have found it actually costs less to work with us over the lifetime of the lease. The real costs you need to manage are vacancy, property damage, evictions, and surprises. Managing those well is exactly why we're the 'no fear' option, and ultimately the less expensive choice."
#### "It's more than I expected to pay."
(Remember: it's about VALUE, not price)
---
"How much were you looking to pay? Let me see what we can do. What price did you have in mind?"
#### "I need to think about it."
---
"Absolutely: what specifically do you need to think through? Is it the pricing, the services, or something else? I want to make sure I've answered everything before we wrap up."
#### "I need to discuss it with my partner/spouse."
---
"Of course: when is a good time for the three of us to hop on a quick call together? I want to make sure all your questions get answered."
#### "We're not ready yet."
---
"I understand. What timeframe are you looking to rent out the property? And when you are ready, what's most important to you in a property manager?"
#### "We're just looking right now."
---
"That's smart: you should look thoroughly before making any decision. Out of curiosity, what are you looking for in a property management company?"
#### "A friend/family member is a property manager."
---
"I completely understand: most people have someone they feel obligated to. But I'd ask: have you considered that mixing business with personal relationships can add stress on both sides? It can be hard to ask the tough questions or hold someone accountable when it's someone you care about. Would you be open to at least comparing what we offer?"
#### "Bring me a tenant first, then we'll talk."
---
"We might have a tenant, but we won't know until we've seen the inside of your home. When would be a good time for us to take a look? Wednesday or Thursday evening?"
#### "Your contract is too complicated."
---
"Whoever you hire to manage your property is responsible for protecting your asset and keeping you out of legal trouble. Every clause in our agreement is there to protect you. We treat your property as if it were our own, and our goal is to help you grow your portfolio and achieve a higher return, not just collect rent."
#### "We're taking it off the market."
---
"I hear you, and if yesterday I brought you a well-qualified tenant, you'd still rent it, right? Let's just meet for 15 minutes. When you see our plan, if it makes sense, great, and if it doesn't, it was just 15 minutes. Sound fair?"
#### "What's your fee?" (asked too early)
---
"Great question, and I want to give you a meaningful answer. It really depends on what you're looking for. Can I ask you a few quick questions about the property first so I can give you the right number?"
#### "I don't want students / college tenants."
---
"We completely understand wanting quality tenants who will care for your property. However, limiting applicants based on age can conflict with fair housing law: [LEGAL: confirm applicable fair housing statutes for your state/market]. What we do instead is run a rigorous 14-point screening process. We also don't allow subletting; each occupant must be individually approved. That alone eliminates the vast majority of problematic applicants."
#### "I don't want pets."
---
"That's a common concern. Here's what we do: we run every pet through a formal pet screening process. We also collect a pet fee each month that goes directly to you as added protection. And our property protection program covers up to $[AMOUNT] in damage above the security deposit. Between the monthly pet fee and our damage program, we've taken the fear out of pets entirely."
#### "My property needs a lot of work."
---
"There are two ways we can approach this. One: we can manage the rehab for you, acting as your point person with our vendor network to drive the work forward. We charge [X]% on invoices under $3K and a flat fee for anything over. Two: you handle the work on your end and we pick up from there once it's rent-ready. Which approach works better for your situation?"
#### "We're not interested."
---
"Of course you're not interested: I haven't given you enough information to make an informed decision yet. Let me just ask you one quick question…" (redirect to a discovery question)

### Section 9: What a Healthy Pipeline Looks Like by Portfolio Size
These are industry-calibrated benchmarks. Adjust based on your market, lead volume, and close rate. A healthy pipeline always has 3 to 5x your monthly door goal in active opportunities.
#### Benchmark Conversion Ratios (Universal)
| Funnel Stage | Benchmark |
|---|---|
| Leads → Discovery Call Completed | 50 to 60% |
| Discovery Call → Listing Appointment Set | 60 to 70% |
| Listing Appointment → PMA Signed (same call) | 40 to 60% |
| Overall Lead → Close Rate | 20 to 30% |
| Average doors per new owner | 1.3 to 1.8 |
---
Rule of Thumb: For every 10 qualified leads, expect 2 to 3 signed PMAs. If you're below that, the breakdown is usually at the listing appointment stage: revisit your pricing presentation and objection handling.
#### Small Portfolio (Under 150 Doors)
Growth Target: 5 to 10 new doors/month
BDM: Owner or part-time BDM wearing multiple hats
| Pipeline Metric | Healthy Number |
|---|---|
| Active leads in CRM | 20 to 35 |
| Discovery calls scheduled/week | 3 to 5 |
| Listing appointments/week | 2 to 3 |
| PMAs signed/month | 3 to 6 |
| Pipeline value (doors) | 15 to 30 active opportunities |
Warning Signs:
- Fewer than 10 active leads in CRM at any time
- No listing appointments in a given week
- Relying on 1 lead source only (usually referrals)
#### Mid-Size Portfolio (150 to 400 Doors)
Growth Target: 10 to 20 new doors/month
BDM: Dedicated full-time BDM
| Pipeline Metric | Healthy Number |
|---|---|
| Active leads in CRM | 50 to 80 |
| Discovery calls completed/week | 6 to 10 |
| Listing appointments/week | 4 to 6 |
| PMAs signed/month | 6 to 12 |
| Pipeline value (doors) | 40 to 70 active opportunities |
| Lead sources active simultaneously | 3 to 5 |
Warning Signs:
- BDM spending more than 20% of time on non-sales tasks
- Pipeline dominated by a single source (e.g., only inbound web)
- Appointment-to-close ratio below 35%
#### Large Portfolio (400+ Doors)
Growth Target: 20 to 40+ new doors/month
BDM: Dedicated BDM + possible SDR/ISA support for lead qualification
| Pipeline Metric | Healthy Number |
|---|---|
| Active leads in CRM | 100 to 150+ |
| Discovery calls completed/week | 12 to 18 |
| Listing appointments/week | 8 to 12 |
| PMAs signed/month | 12 to 20+ |
| Pipeline value (doors) | 80 to 120+ active opportunities |
| Lead sources active simultaneously | 5 to 7+ |
Warning Signs:
- BDM close rate dropping (burnout or poor lead quality)
- Ops team overwhelmed by onboarding volume: signal to hire
- No nurture sequence for leads that didn't close in 30 to 60 days
- CRM not updated: pipeline data is unreliable
#### Universal Pipeline Health Indicators (Any Size)
| Healthy | Unhealthy |
|---|---|
| Multiple active lead sources | Single lead source dependency |
| CRM updated daily | CRM updated "when I remember" |
| Every deal has a next action + date | Deals sitting with no follow-up task |
| Appointment show rate above 80% | Frequent no-shows or reschedules |
| Referrals represent 20 to 30% of leads | Zero referral activity |
| Stalled deals reviewed weekly | Stalled deals ignored for weeks |
| BDM closes 40%+ of listing appointments | Close rate below 30% |
### Section 10: The BDM Cadence: Daily, Weekly and Monthly
#### Daily Cadence: Time Block Structure (Recommended)
| Time | Activity |
|---|---|
| 7:30 to 8:00 AM | CRM review: check all tasks due today, pipeline updates, overnight inbound leads |
| 8:00 to 8:30 AM | Speed-to-lead: respond to ALL new inbound leads from prior evening/morning inside the configured {{speed_to_lead_minutes}}-minute window |
| 8:30 to 11:30 AM | Power Prospecting Block: outbound calls (FRBO, older leads, referral follow-ups, nurture sequences) |
| 11:30 AM to 12:00 PM | CRM updates from morning calls; schedule any new appointments |
| 12:00 to 1:00 PM | Lunch / relationship-building (agent lunches, investor calls, networking) |
| 1:00 to 4:00 PM | Listing appointments, discovery calls, pre-appointment nurture touches |
| 4:00 to 4:30 PM | Post-appointment follow-up: emails, texts, CRM notes within 5 to 15 min of each call |
| 4:30 to 5:00 PM | End-of-day CRM cleanup: log all activity, set tomorrow's tasks, update pipeline stages |
#### Daily Non-Negotiables
- Respond to every new inbound lead inside the configured {{speed_to_lead_minutes}}-minute window
- Make minimum [X] outbound prospecting calls (set your own daily floor; suggested: 10 to 20)
- Update CRM after every interaction: no exceptions
- Send at least 1 post-call email or text follow-up sequence
- Check for any listing appointment reminders due tomorrow (call to "remind," not confirm)
#### Weekly Cadence
Monday: Plan and Prioritize
- Review full pipeline: every deal, every stage
- Set weekly appointment goal and prospecting targets
- Identify top 5 "hot" deals most likely to close this week
- Pull new FRBO listings for outbound prospecting
- Confirm all listing appointments scheduled for the week
Tuesday to Thursday: Execute
- Full daily cadence (see above)
- Conduct listing appointments
- Run pre-appointment nurture sequences (24-hr call, morning-of text)
- Send value-add touches to nurture leads (market updates, leased-in-your-neighborhood emails)
- Follow up on any unsigned PMAs from prior appointments
Friday: Review and Reconnect
- Weekly pipeline review with manager/owner (30 to 45 min; see Section 8)
- Log all PMAs signed this week; trigger handoff workflow
- Send end-of-week nurture emails to stalled leads
- Identify lead sources that produced this week vs. those that didn't
- Set Monday's priority list before logging off

#### Monthly Cadence
Week 1: Kickoff
- Set monthly door goal and reverse-engineer the activity needed to hit it
- Review prior month's close rate, lead sources, and lost deals
- Launch any new outbound campaigns (direct mail, FRBO blitz, agent outreach)
- Refresh nurture sequences for leads 30, 60, 90+ days old
Week 2 to 3: Execute and Adjust
- Mid-month pipeline check: on track to hit goal?
- If behind: increase outbound volume, add a lead source, or revisit stalled deals
- Relationship-building: schedule 2 to 4 agent/investor coffees or calls this month
- Request referrals from recently onboarded owners (30-day mark is ideal)
Week 4: Close and Report
- Push hard on any deals in listing appointment or post-appointment stage
- Finalize all PMAs; ensure all handoffs to operations are clean
- Complete monthly report for leadership (see metrics below)
- Conduct lost-deal debrief: what objection killed it? What could have been done differently?

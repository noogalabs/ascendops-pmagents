# The Discovery Call

The call where you find out whether there is a real deal here. Not the call where you explain what the company does.

Read `question-led-selling` first if you have not this session. The question bank below is *what* to ask; the method is *how*, and the how is what makes an owner answer the hard ones honestly.

**The ratio:** the owner should be talking most of the time. If you have said more than they have, this was a pitch with questions in it.

---

## Property Basics
- Where is the property? Address, city, state, zip
- Tell me about it — bedrooms, baths, square footage, stories, pool?
- Primary residence, second home, or investment property?
- When did you buy it?
- Are you living there now, or is there a tenant in place?

→ Board: C1–C16

## The Financial Picture
- What are the carrying costs — mortgage, insurance, taxes, HOA?
- What does the HOA cover, if there is one?
- What are you hoping to rent it for?
- Have you thought about selling it instead?

→ Board: C11, C12, E8, E9

> That last question is not a distraction. An owner who is genuinely torn between renting and selling is an owner whose timeline can evaporate in a week, and it is better to know now.

## Motivation and Timeline
- What are you looking to accomplish with this property — short term and long term?
- How soon would you want a tenant in place?
- How soon is the home available?
- Have you rented this one, or any property, before?
- If so, did you work with a manager? What did you like, and what did you not?

→ Board: E1–E4

## Competitive Intelligence
- Have you spoken with any other management companies?
- What did you like or not like about them?
- Are they a dedicated management company, or an agent doing it on the side?
- If they are leaving a current manager: why?
- What matters most to you in a management company?

→ Board: D6, D7, D8

> "What matters most to you" is the highest-value question in the bank. Whatever they say is the thing to lead with at the appointment — and if you skip it you will lead with the thing that matters most to *you* instead.

## Decision-Making
- Are you working with an agent on a purchase or sale of this property?
- Do you own other properties?
- Are you buying, selling, or leasing anything else right now?
- **Who else is involved in this decision?** Spouse, partner, another member of the entity?
- When are you looking to make a decision?

→ Board: E11, E12, B3, B11

> **B11 is a gate, not a data point.** An appointment where a decision-maker is missing does not count as held, and the follow-up conversation starts over from the beginning. Ask it here, confirm it before the appointment, and confirm it again the morning of.

## The Prevention Questions

Ask every one of these before an agreement is sent. Each maps to a surprise that otherwise shows up during onboarding, when the company is already committed <!-- judgment §7 -->.

| Ask | Catches |
|---|---|
| Is there an HOA? Any rental restrictions or approval requirements? | An HOA rental cap discovered after signing |
| Is it occupied? If so — do you have the signed lease, the deposit ledger, and the move-in inspection? | Missing tenant documentation, and a deposit you cannot defend later <!-- A7 --> |
| When was the HVAC last serviced? How old is the roof? Any deferred maintenance you know about? | Undisclosed capital problems |
| Is the mortgage current? | Foreclosure risk |
| What is the exact legal name of the ownership entity, and can you provide the documentation? | An entity mismatch that can make the agreement unenforceable <!-- A5 --> |
| Any open insurance claims on the property? | Claims that change condition and coverage |
| Any code violations or city notices in the last two years? | Violations that make placing a tenant a liability |
| Any pending legal matter involving the property or the current tenant? | Litigation the company would be inheriting |

**Three of these are stop conditions, not notes.** Missing deposit documentation, an entity mismatch, and any violation or pending legal matter go to `property-and-owner-gates` before an agreement is sent — not after, and not "we'll sort it during onboarding".

**Never ask what kind of tenant the owner is looking for.** The question invites the answer that ends the deal, and once it is said it has to be logged and routed. See `fair-housing-guard`.

## Writing It Down

Within minutes of hanging up, not at end of day:

- **E2, E3, G10 in the owner's own words.** Not your paraphrase. "It's eating my weekends" is usable at the appointment; "owner is frustrated with self-management" is not, and you cannot recover the first from the second.
- E5 motivation — your honest read, hot / warm / cold. An inflated E5 sorts a dead deal to the top of tomorrow's day view every morning until someone notices.
- E6 property type fit
- E10 once the analysis is run — always as a range, always with its pull date
- F1, F2, then F15 and F16

Then move the stage and update A6. See `stage-gates`.

---

## The Qualifying Close

When the gates are clear and the motivation is real, book from inside the conversation while the awareness is highest:

```
Great — the next step is just getting time on the calendar so I can put together
a real rental analysis and walk you through what this looks like. Would [day] or
[day] work better?
```

Two named options, not an open question. And confirm in the same breath who else will be on that call.

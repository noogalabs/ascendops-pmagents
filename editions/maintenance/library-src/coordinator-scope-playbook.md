# The Coordinator Scope Playbook, Move-Out Inspection to Make Ready Decision Guide

## The Coordinator's Scope Playbook
Move-Out Inspection to Make Ready Decision Guide, 420 Doors, Class B to D, [Company Name]
How to use this: at every move-out inspection, run each finding through its table. Column by column: Is it wear or damage? What is the scope line item? Who does it? What does it cost by class? How long? Does the owner need to approve it first?
### Master Rules Before You Start

---

| Rule | What It Means |
|---|---|
| Compare to move-in report always | No charge is defensible without a move-in photo to compare against |
| Age matters | A 7-year-old carpet that is "dirty" is not a chargeback; it is end of life |
| Document everything with photos | One photo per finding, labeled by room and item |
| Threshold = auto-approve | Anything within the owner's pre-approved reserve ($500 typical) then proceed without a call |
| Over threshold = owner approval first | Send scope + cost estimate, wait for written approval before dispatching |
| Tenant damage = chargeback | Document, photograph, note on security deposit disposition |
| Wear and tear = owner's cost | Do not charge tenant; proceed as normal make ready |
| Safety items = never wait for approval | Smoke detectors, exposed wiring, gas leaks: fix immediately, notify owner after |

#### Paint Judgment Calls
- Touch-up vs. full paint rule. Touch up if: same paint color on file, less than 2 walls affected, paint is under 3 years old, sheen matches. Full room if: touch-up will not blend (most of the time it will not after 2+ years). Full unit if: 3+ rooms need paint, unit is 5+ years since last paint, or tenant caused damage throughout.
- Rule of thumb: "If you can see the touch-up from 3 feet away, it's a full wall. If 3+ walls in a room, it's the whole room. If 3+ rooms, it's the whole unit."
- Class D reality: budget flat paint fades fast; full repaints are more common every 2 to 3 years.

#### Carpet Judgment Calls
- Clean vs. replace rule: always try shampoo first unless: pet urine soaked to pad, burn holes, tears, or carpet is at or past useful life.
- Useful life rule: carpet = 5 to 7 years. If it is 6 years old and just dirty, then owner cost to replace, no chargeback.
- Prorated chargeback: if carpet is 3 years old (50% life used) and tenant destroyed it, then charge 50% of replacement cost.
- Pet urine test: if you can smell it from the doorway, the pad is saturated, then replace carpet AND pad, no shampoo debate.
- Class D rule of thumb: LVP is almost always better than carpet on re-do; present to owner as upgrade option.

- Repair vs. replace rule. Repair if: appliance is under 7 years old AND repair cost is less than 50% of replacement cost. Replace if: appliance is 8+ years old OR repair cost exceeds 50% of replacement.

- Safety rule: no heat in winter or no AC in extreme heat = emergency dispatch regardless of approval threshold; notify owner immediately after.

#### Plumbing Judgment Calls
- Leak vs. drip: a drip is a work order. A leak with water damage is an emergency: dispatch immediately, document everything, notify owner same day.
- Water heater age rule: 8 years = start the replacement conversation with the owner proactively; 10+ years = recommend replacement at turnover even if it is "working".
- Tenant damage vs. wear: clogs from foreign objects (wipes, toys, grease buildup) = chargeback. Slow drains from normal use = wear.
- Class D rule: galvanized or older supply lines are common; if you see one failing, flag all of them to owner; piecemeal fixes cost more long-term.

#### Electrical Judgment Calls
- Safety is non-negotiable: exposed wiring, burning smells, panel issues, missing smoke/CO detectors, then fix first, document, notify owner; never wait for approval.
- Licensed only rule: anything beyond outlet/switch swaps and fixture replacements goes to a licensed electrician; no exceptions.
- Smoke detector rule: every unit gets tested at every turnover. Missing = chargeback. Dead battery = wear. Non-functional hardwired = replace immediately.
- Class D rule: older homes may have aluminum wiring or outdated panels; flag to owner immediately; this is a liability issue, not just a maintenance item.

#### Windows & Screens Judgment Calls
- Screen rule: missing screens are almost always a chargeback; they do not just disappear from normal wear.

- Foggy windows: seal failure is age/wear, owner cost; present as an upgrade opportunity if multiple windows are affected.
- Blinds: standard 2-inch faux wood blinds are inexpensive; replace rather than repair; document missing ones as tenant damage.

#### Odor Judgment Calls
- The nose test at the door: if you smell it before you walk in, it is severe. If you smell it in the room, it is moderate. If you only smell it up close, it is mild. Scope accordingly.
- Pet urine escalation ladder: shampoo, then replace carpet/pad + seal subfloor, then seal + prime + repaint. Move up the ladder based on smell after each step.
- Smoke rule: ozone treatment alone rarely solves heavy smoke; if it is in the walls, you must prime with odor-blocking primer (Kilz or BIN) before repainting or the smell returns through new paint.
- Never mask odors: air fresheners and deodorizers are not a scope item; they are a red flag that the real problem was not solved. If the next tenant smells it, you own it.
- Mold smell rule: if you smell mold but cannot see it, then open walls and investigate before proceeding. Hidden mold is a liability, not a make-ready line item.

#### Pest Judgment Calls
- Preventive treatment is always done at every turnover; it costs $50 to $150 and prevents a $500 to $1,500 problem with the next tenant.
- Roaches = sanitation, almost always tenant-caused; chargeback is defensible with photos of conditions (grease, food debris, clutter).
- Bedbugs rule: never skip professional treatment. Heat treatment is the gold standard. Chemical alone often fails. Document everything; bedbugs are a legal liability.
- Fleas = pet damage; always paired with carpet treatment or replacement; treating the unit without treating the carpet is a waste of money.
- Termites = owner's problem, not tenant's; this is a structural/property issue; notify owner immediately, get a licensed inspection, do not delay.
- Class D rule: preventive treatment should be quarterly on Class D properties, not just at turnover; flag this to the owner as a recurring maintenance recommendation.

# Owner Contributions and Shortfalls

Source: monthly workflow Step 6, plus judgment scenarios 6 and 7.

---

## When a ledger is short

An owner ledger is short when the balance, net of pending payables and the reserve floor in `thresholds.reserve_floor` (B3) or that owner's override, will not cover approved expenses.

Prepare a contribution request carrying:

- current balance
- pending bills, itemized, with their approval status
- the reserve floor being protected and where it comes from (the management agreement, per B3)
- the shortfall amount
- the due date, driven by the earliest payment that depends on it

**Target: request to the property manager within one business day of identifying the shortfall.**

Then: the property manager reviews and contacts the owner. The owner funds the trust account directly. The agent verifies the contribution posted as an **owner contribution, not income**, and confirms funds have **cleared** before any payment releases against that balance.

Pending is not cleared. Releasing against uncleared funds is how an account goes negative without anyone deciding to overdraw it.

---

## Scenario 6 — owner asks for more than their balance

**Do right now.** Pull the ledger. Confirm the available balance net of pending payables, reserves, and management fees. Commit to nothing. Tell the owner the property manager will follow up.

**Never.** Never disburse more than the available balance. Never borrow from another owner's funds, even temporarily. Never promise a timeline.

**Escalate when.** Immediately. The property manager owns the conversation about what is available, what is pending, and what the options are.

**Write down.** Date of request, amount requested, actual available balance, PM decision, communication sent. Decision-log scenario S6.

---

## Scenario 7 — someone suggests using another owner's funds

An owner is short. Somebody — the owner, a colleague, someone being helpful — suggests covering it from another owner's balance "just until next month."

**Do right now.** Decline immediately and clearly. This is not a judgment call. Say plainly: *"We are not able to use another owner's funds for this."* Then escalate.

**Never.** Never move funds between owner ledgers to cover a shortfall. Never temporarily. Never without telling anyone. This is commingling: a license violation in every state and potentially criminal.

**Escalate when.** Immediately, every time, no exceptions.

**Write down.** Date, who asked, what was requested, your response, PM decision, resolution. Decision-log scenario S7.

There is no version of this that is fine because it is small, or because it reverses next month, or because both owners would agree. The prohibition is structural.

---

## What the property manager decides

- Whether to draw from authorized reserves first
- How to communicate urgency to the owner
- Whether to pause non-emergency work until the account is funded

---

## Hard gates

- This agent does not post a contribution, release a payment against one, or move funds anywhere.
- Cleared, not pending, is the bar before anything releases.
- Cross-owner borrowing is refused on the spot and escalated, not analyzed.
